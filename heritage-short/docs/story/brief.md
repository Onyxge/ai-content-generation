# Story brief (public version)

Working title: The Last Pour. Drawn from Y's script of 2026-11-26, which is inspired by a story Y's grandmother tells. The dialogue and the specific events are imagined; the grandmother's memory is that a man in the family's story spent his life serving the emperor. In the film the emperor is never named: he is "the great emperor".

## The story in three to five sentences

For years, a young man served honey wine (tej) at the palace of the great emperor. He knew how to pour without spilling a drop, and how to disappear into a room. One night, at a formal gathering, a guest mocks him by asking whether the emperor even knows his name; he lowers his eyes and says nothing. The emperor, who noticed, sets down his glass, asks the server his name, hears "Tesfaye", and raises a toast to him, and the whole room lifts its glasses. Later, alone, Tesfaye pours the last of the honey wine, takes one small sip, and looks toward the palace lights.

## Who is in it

- **Tesfaye, the honey wine server.** A fictional character inspired by the family story, not a portrait of a real person (real_person: false). A young Ethiopian man, slim, close-cropped hair, in a plain white cotton server's tunic and trousers, careful, steady hands; quiet, watchful, dignified.
- **A guest.** Fictional. An older nobleman at the gathering, in a dark cape over white, with a thin smile.
- **The great emperor.** Never named. In the film he is a fictional ruler of the imperial era, not a portrait of any real person (real_person: false): a short, elderly Ethiopian man with a very upright, composed posture and a calm, attentive gaze, in a plain dark formal cloak. He is never voiced; the narrator tells what he says. Frame him at a distance, from behind or in profile, presiding at the head of the table; his face is never in close-up, so the picture stays about Tesfaye and never becomes a likeness.
- **The grandmother** who tells the story. She is mentioned by the narrator but never shown or voiced.

The narrator is a prebuilt voice speaking as the grandchild who heard the story from the grandmother. The narrator retells everything anyone says ("A guest asked whether the emperor even knew his name"). No character, and no real person, is given a voice.

## The place

An imperial palace in Addis Ababa in the imperial era: a palace kitchen before dawn, lit by lamps; a formal banquet hall with a long table, white cloths, candles and warm lamplight; a quiet corridor or doorway at night looking out at the palace lights. The honey wine is golden tej, poured from a flask into a berele, the small round-bellied glass flask it is drunk from.

## The moment it should land on

The emperor raising a toast to the server by his name, Tesfaye, and the tear Tesfaye tries to hide. The last line: for one moment, the whole room remembered the man behind the tray.

## Words that must be said, or must not be

- Say: Tesfaye (TES-fah-yay), honey wine, "the man behind the tray".
- Open close to: "For years, he served the emperor. They called him the honey wine server."
- End close to: "My grandmother said he spent his life serving the emperor. But that night, for one moment, the whole room remembered the man behind the tray."
- Don't: name the emperor, put words in his voice, show his face in close-up, or present the imagined scenes as documented history.
